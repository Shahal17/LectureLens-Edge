# Student User Pilot Guide

Use the same 5 to 10 minute lecture clip for all participants. Obtain the
lecturer's and participants' permission before recording or sharing content.
Prefer an openly licensed or synthetic clip for early testing.

## Session checklist

1. Explain what the app processes and what it stores.
2. Ask the participant to analyse the transcript without coaching.
3. Observe where the participant hesitates or needs help.
4. Ask the participant to inspect at least one source marker.
5. Test one supported and one unsupported question.
6. Ask the participant to try the quiz and one accessibility control.
7. Delete or return any participant data after the agreed research period.

## Rating questions

Use a scale from 1, strongly disagree, to 5, strongly agree.

1. The summary captured the lecture's main ideas. `1 2 3 4 5`
2. Source markers made the notes more trustworthy. `1 2 3 4 5`
3. Confusion Radar highlighted material worth reviewing. `1 2 3 4 5`
4. The quiz helped me notice what I had not understood. `1 2 3 4 5`
5. I would use this when internet access is limited. `1 2 3 4 5`
6. I understood what stayed on my device. `1 2 3 4 5`
7. I could complete the main task without assistance. `1 2 3 4 5`

## Short-answer questions

- Which feature was most useful?
- Which output was incorrect or confusing?
- At what point did you hesitate?
- What would make you use this every week?
- Which subject would benefit most?
- May one anonymised sentence from your feedback be quoted in public project
  updates?

## Research notes

Separate observations from conclusions. For example:

- Observation: three participants looked for the source marker before reading
  the answer.
- Interpretation: evidence visibility may influence trust.
- Next test: compare always-visible markers with markers shown on demand.

Five participants can reveal usability problems, but they do not establish
population-level accuracy or learning impact.
